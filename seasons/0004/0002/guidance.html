# Design direction

Treat the page as a system of information with a clear first read and a useful second read. Establish hierarchy through placement, scale, spacing, and contrast; make the challenge, rules, standings, and individual entries easy to distinguish without flattening them into interchangeable cards.

Choose a typographic approach with a point of view. Consider less expected pairings, locally available system faces, or a deliberate use of weight and measure. Let type do structural work as well as atmospheric work, and keep long passages comfortable to read.

Make each visual choice feel connected to the same idea. Use a restrained set of colours, shapes, alignments, and repeated details, then introduce variation where the content gives you a reason. Look for an original composition or visual metaphor that belongs to this page rather than borrowing a familiar dashboard or publication template wholesale.

Protect legibility and access as you experiment: preserve meaningful content, strong text contrast, visible focus where interactive controls exist, sensible line lengths, and a clear reading order. Distinctive work can still be calm, direct, and easy to use.
